Golden Anniversary Coverage

Synagogue celebrates its 50th anniversary

Salem News · October 3, 1972 · Byline unknown

Source: CTI historical scrapbook. The surviving clipping is identified in the scrapbook by the handwritten notation “Oct. 3 1972 Salem News.”

PEABODY — In 1911, a small band of Jews came to this area from Turkey.

These people, who traced their lineage back to pre-Christian Spain, formed the original congregation of Tifereth Israel, on Pierpont St., which celebrates its 50th anniversary this past weekend.

Special synagogue services included memorial prayers for Mrs. Vickie Freedman, recently deceased president of the synagogue Women's Auxiliary. Also held was installation of officers. Installed were: President, Joseph Gibely II; vice president, Isaac Hasday; secretary, Victor Havian, and treasurer, Jack Yonis.

A dinner-dance was held Sunday to culminate the weekend festivities.

Congregants of the Tifereth Israel Synagogue are descended from Spanish (Sephardic) Jews who lived in that country prior to the fall of Jerusalem in 70 B.C. There they flourished and numbered among them physicians, bankers, skilled artisans and the greatest of Jewish scholars. The Spanish Inquisition (about the time of the discovery of America) saw the Jews dispersed throughout western Europe and Palestine.

The ancestors of the Peabody Congregation traveled to Turkey, where they took their places among the workers of that community.

The lure of America gripped the Sephardic Jew of Turkey and in 1911 the first of their number, Leon Eskenas and Myer Benater arrived in this area where they were soon joined by their wives Rebecca Eskenas and Regine Benater.

Shortly thereafter, the Gibely, Eskenazi, Yonis, Hasday, Leon, Havian, Pernitchi, Savy, Fermon and Erera families arrived and from this small group the sephardic synagogue, Congregation Tifereth Israel of Peabody and Salem emerged. (This synagogue is the only sephardic congregation north of Rhode Island.)

The Sephardic Jews have retained many of the customs acquired during the years their ancestors spent in Spain and the congregants speak a fluent “Ladino” (Spanish) despite the fact that not one of their number has set foot on the Spanish mainland in almost 5 centuries.

Tifereth Israel has never numbered more than 35 adult male members, although this number has remained constant and active in the 50 years since the congregation's inception. With the exception of the Leon, Erera and Menater families, whose descendants have moved out of the Salem-Peabody area, the children and grand-children of the congregation founders still remain active members of the synagogue.

Of the original group who arrived here in 1911, only Rebecca Eskenas is alive today. She is living in retirement in Florida.
